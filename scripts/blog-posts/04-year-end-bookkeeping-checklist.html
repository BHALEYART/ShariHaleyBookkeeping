Short answer: before the year closes, make sure every account is reconciled, every transaction is categorized, payroll and contractor records are complete, and you can hand your CPA a clean profit and loss statement and balance sheet. The checklist below walks through it step by step.

Year-end is when small bookkeeping gaps turn into big tax-season headaches. A few hours of review now can save weeks of back-and-forth with your CPA later.

1. Reconcile every account

  • Bank accounts, through the last statement of the year
  • Credit cards
  • Loans and lines of credit (compare balances to lender statements)
  • Payment processors and merchant accounts

2. Clean up transactions

  • Categorize anything left in “uncategorized” or “ask my accountant”
  • Look for duplicates and transactions posted to the wrong year
  • Separate personal expenses from business expenses
  • Make sure large purchases (equipment, vehicles) are recorded clearly so your CPA can treat them correctly

3. Review receivables and payables

  • Follow up on unpaid customer invoices
  • Flag invoices that likely won’t be collected, and ask your CPA how to handle them
  • Confirm unpaid bills are accurate and current

4. Payroll and contractors

  • Confirm employee names, addresses, and Social Security numbers are correct for W-2s
  • Review year-to-date payroll totals against your payroll reports
  • Collect a W-9 from every contractor you paid, ideally before January
  • Total payments to each contractor so 1099-NEC forms are ready; they’re generally due by January 31

Tip: collecting W-9s at the start of a working relationship, not at year-end, makes January far less stressful.

5. Organize your documents

  • Year-end bank, credit card, and loan statements
  • Receipts for significant expenses
  • Records of estimated tax payments made during the year
  • Asset purchase and sale paperwork

Not sure what to keep, or for how long? See how long to keep business records.

6. Run your year-end reports

Once everything is reconciled, run your profit and loss statement and balance sheet for the year. Review them for anything that looks off, then send them to your CPA along with the documents they request.

7. Meet with your CPA

Ideally before December 31. Clean books give you and your CPA time to talk about planning, not just paperwork.
