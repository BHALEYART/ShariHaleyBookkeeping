Short answer: the IRS’s general rule is to keep records that support your tax return for three years, and to keep employment tax (payroll) records for at least four years. Some records need to be kept longer, so check with your CPA about your situation.

Good recordkeeping isn’t just about tax time. Organized records make it easier to track your business’s progress, prepare financial statements, and answer questions quickly if they ever come up.

What the IRS says

  • General rule: three years. According to the IRS, the general rule is three years, depending on the action, expense, and event the document records.
  • Employment tax records: at least four years. The IRS says business owners should keep all records of employment taxes for at least four years.
  • Some situations call for longer. The IRS describes cases where records should be kept longer, and records for property and assets are commonly kept until well after you sell or dispose of them.

Read the IRS’s own guidance: Common questions about recordkeeping for small businesses and Recordkeeping.

What records to keep

  • Income: invoices, sales records, deposit records, and 1099s you receive
  • Expenses: receipts, bills, canceled checks, and credit card statements
  • Bank and loan statements
  • Payroll: timesheets, payroll reports, and payroll tax filings
  • Assets: purchase and sale records for equipment, vehicles, and property
  • Tax returns and the reports used to prepare them

A simple way to stay organized

  1. Create one folder per year, with subfolders for income, expenses, payroll, banking, and assets.
  2. Attach receipts to transactions in QuickBooks as you go, so the backup lives with the entry.
  3. Reconcile every month, so statements and books always match.
  4. At year-end, save your final reports and statements together (see the year-end checklist).
  5. Calendar a yearly review of what can be safely discarded, after checking with your CPA.
