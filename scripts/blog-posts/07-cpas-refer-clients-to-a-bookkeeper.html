Short answer: when your clients have a dedicated bookkeeper, their books arrive at tax time current, reconciled, and categorized. That means fewer hours on cleanup, fewer follow-up questions, and more of your time spent on tax work and advisory, which is the work your clients value most.

Every CPA knows the client who shows up in March with a year of uncategorized transactions. Referring those clients to a bookkeeper you trust helps them, and it helps your firm.

What changes when your clients have a bookkeeper

  • Reconciled accounts: bank and credit card accounts reconciled every month, not reconstructed at year-end
  • Consistent categorization: transactions coded the same way all year, so the trial balance makes sense
  • Payroll that ties out: payroll records consistent with the general ledger
  • Organized backup: receipts and documents attached and easy to find
  • Year-end reports on time: P&L and balance sheet ready when you need them

Why it’s good for your firm

  • Less write-up and cleanup work during your busiest weeks
  • Fewer extensions driven by missing or messy records
  • Better conversations with clients about planning, not paperwork
  • A clear division of labor: the bookkeeper handles the transactional side; you keep the tax and advisory relationship

Your client stays your client. A good bookkeeping partner supports your relationship with the client. Shari doesn’t prepare tax returns; she makes sure the books you receive are ready for you.

Clients who are a good fit for a referral

  • Small businesses that are months (or years) behind
  • Clients whose books need a clean-up after a previous bookkeeper
  • Growing businesses that have added payroll
  • Owners who want to keep books in-house but need QuickBooks training to do it right
  • Clients applying for loans who need current financial statements
