Short answer: a bookkeeper records and organizes your financial transactions all year. An accountant analyzes those records and prepares financial reports and advice. A CPA is an accountant licensed by the state, who can do things like represent you before the IRS and perform audits. Most small businesses do best with a bookkeeper and a CPA working together.

The three titles get used interchangeably, but the jobs are different. Knowing who does what helps you hire the right help, and avoid paying CPA rates for bookkeeping work.

What a bookkeeper does

Bookkeepers focus on the transactional side of your business: making sure every dollar in and out is recorded correctly and on time.

  • Recording and categorizing transactions
  • Reconciling bank and credit card accounts
  • Managing invoices, bills, and payroll
  • Keeping receipts and backup documents organized
  • Producing regular reports like your profit and loss statement and balance sheet

Good bookkeeping is consistent, careful work done every month. It’s the foundation everything else is built on.

What an accountant does

Accountants focus on analysis, reporting, and decision-making. They interpret your financial records, prepare financial statements, and help with budgeting, forecasting, and tax strategy. Some accountants also prepare tax returns.

What a CPA does

A Certified Public Accountant has passed the CPA exam and met their state’s education, experience, and licensing requirements. In addition to accounting work, CPAs can provide services that require a license, such as audits and certain attest work, and can represent clients before the IRS. Many small businesses work with a CPA for tax returns and tax planning.

How they work together

Think of it as a relay. The bookkeeper keeps the books accurate all year, and the CPA uses those books at tax time and for bigger-picture advice. When the books arrive clean and reconciled, the CPA can spend their time on what you hired them for, rather than reconstructing your year.

Great accounting starts with great bookkeeping. Clean, current books make your CPA’s work faster, your tax return more accurate, and financing applications easier.

Which one do you need?

  • Your books are behind or messy: start with a bookkeeper. Catch-up or clean-up bookkeeping gets you current.
  • You need ongoing records and payroll: a bookkeeper on a monthly or quarterly schedule.
  • You need tax returns, tax planning, or IRS representation: a CPA, working from the books your bookkeeper maintains.
