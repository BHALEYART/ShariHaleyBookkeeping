What a bookkeeper can (and can’t) do for your CPA firm’s clients

A clear division of labor for CPAs: what a bookkeeping partner handles for your clients, what stays with your firm, and how the year-end handoff works.

By Shari Haley, QuickBooks Platinum Certified ProAdvisor · November 23, 2026 · 2 min read

Short answer: a bookkeeper handles the transactional side of your client’s business all year: recording, categorizing, reconciling, payroll, and reports. Tax returns, tax planning, and advisory work stay with your firm. A clear division of labor means cleaner books for you and no confusion for the client.

What the bookkeeper handles

  • Recording and categorizing transactions throughout the year
  • Monthly bank and credit card reconciliations
  • Invoicing, bills, and receivables and payables
  • Payroll processing and payroll records
  • Organizing receipts and backup documents
  • Monthly or quarterly financial reports
  • Catch-up and clean-up of books that are behind or inaccurate
  • Training owners and staff to use QuickBooks correctly

What stays with your firm

  • Tax returns and tax filings
  • Tax planning and tax advice
  • Representation before the IRS
  • Audits, reviews, and other attest work
  • Year-end adjusting entries you choose to make

Questions go to the right person. When a client asks a tax question, a good bookkeeping partner points them back to you rather than answering it.

A smooth year-end handoff

  1. Agree on what you want. Reports, format, and timing: P&L, balance sheet, general ledger detail, payroll summaries, or accountant access to the file.
  2. Close the year. All accounts reconciled through December, open items resolved or flagged.
  3. Deliver the package with a short list of anything that needs your judgment.
  4. Post your adjustments. If you make adjusting entries, share them so next year starts from the right balances.

Quick answers

Will a bookkeeper give my client tax advice?

No. Tax advice, planning, and returns stay with you. A bookkeeper keeps the records accurate and flags questions for the client to bring to their CPA.

Can we agree on how the year-end package is delivered?

Yes. Every firm has its own preferences for reports and format. Agreeing on them up front makes the handoff smooth every year.

This article is general information, not tax or legal advice. Every business is different, so check with your CPA or tax professional about your situation.
