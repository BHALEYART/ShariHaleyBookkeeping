Do I need a bookkeeper for my small business? 7 signs it’s time

Not sure if your small business needs a bookkeeper? Here are 7 common signs it’s time to get help, and what a bookkeeper actually does for you.

By Shari Haley, QuickBooks Platinum Certified ProAdvisor · November 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Short answer: if your books are behind, you’re unsure your numbers are right, or bookkeeping is eating time you should spend running the business, it’s probably time. A bookkeeper keeps your records current, accurate, and ready for your CPA, so you can make decisions from real numbers instead of guesses.

Most owners don’t start a business to do bookkeeping. It’s easy to keep up at first, then one busy season later there’s a shoebox of receipts and a bank feed full of uncategorized transactions. Here are the signs we see most often in DeLand and across Volusia County.

1. Your books are more than a month behind

If you’re catching up on transactions from last quarter, your numbers can’t tell you much about today. Books that are months behind also make tax season stressful and expensive, because everything has to be reconstructed at once. If you’re already well behind, catch-up bookkeeping is designed for exactly this.

2. You’re not sure your numbers are right

Uncategorized transactions, duplicate entries, and accounts that have never been reconciled are common in do-it-yourself books. If you look at your profit and loss report and don’t trust it, you can’t use it to price your work, plan purchases, or apply for financing.

3. Tax time is a scramble every year

When your CPA asks for year-end reports and you spend weeks pulling them together, that time is expensive twice: once for you, and again if your CPA has to clean things up before preparing your return. Organized, reconciled books make the handoff simple.

4. You’ve added payroll or employees

Payroll brings deadlines, withholding, and filings that have to be right every time. A bookkeeper who handles payroll keeps wages, deductions, and payroll tax records consistent with the rest of your books.

5. You’re applying for a loan or line of credit

Lenders typically want current financial statements. If producing a clean profit and loss statement and balance sheet would take weeks, getting your books in order first can make the process much smoother.

6. You can’t tell if you’re actually profitable

Money in the bank isn’t the same as profit. Without accurate, categorized books, it’s hard to know which services, products, or customers really make you money. Regular financial reports turn your bookkeeping into decision-making information.

7. Bookkeeping is taking time you don’t have

If you’re doing books late at night or on weekends, compare those hours to what you earn doing your actual work. For many owners, handing bookkeeping off pays for itself in time alone, before you count the mistakes it prevents.

What a bookkeeper does for you

  • Records and categorizes every transaction so your taxes are done right
  • Reconciles bank and credit card accounts every month
  • Organizes receipts and backup documents
  • Runs payroll and keeps payroll records in order
  • Produces regular financial reports you can actually use
  • Keeps your records ready for your CPA, tax returns, and financing applications

Quick answers

Can I do my own bookkeeping?

Many owners do at first, and that can work while the business is small and simple. It usually stops working when transactions pile up, payroll starts, or the books keep falling behind. That’s when the hours you spend (and the mistakes that slip in) cost more than the help would.

How often does a bookkeeper work on my books?

It depends on your volume. Many small businesses do well with monthly bookkeeping; others with fewer transactions choose quarterly. Either way, the goal is books that are current and reconciled when you need them.

Is a bookkeeper the same as an accountant?

No. A bookkeeper keeps your day-to-day financial records accurate and current. An accountant or CPA typically uses those records for tax returns, analysis, and advice. Most businesses benefit from both. Here’s the difference in detail.
